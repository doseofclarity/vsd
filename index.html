Ventricular Septal Defect (VSD)

"A hole in the heart's wall – the most common congenital heart defect." – Pillitteri

VSD Explained Like You Are 5 Years Old

🧸 Imagine your heart is a house with two rooms:

• Left room (Left ventricle) – has blue blood (oxygen‑rich)
• Right room (Right ventricle) – has red blood (oxygen‑poor)
• Between the rooms there is a wall (septum).

🔴 VSD means there is a hole in that wall.

💨 So some of the blue blood (oxygen‑rich) leaks into the red blood side. That means the body doesn't get enough oxygen, and the heart has to work much harder! ❤️💦

✅ The good news: many holes get smaller by themselves as a child grows. Doctors can also fix them with medicine or a small surgery.

Definition & Epidemiology

Ventricular Septal Defect (VSD) – an abnormal opening (hole) in the interventricular septum, allowing blood to flow from the left ventricle to the right ventricle (left‑to‑right shunt).

📊 Frequency
Most common congenital heart defect (25‑30% of all CHD). Occurs in 1.5‑3.5 per 1,000 live births.
🇵🇭 Philippines
VSD accounts for ~30% of all congenital heart disease cases in Philippine hospitals. Estimated 4,000‑5,000 affected newborns annually.
⚠️ Key point: Small VSDs may close spontaneously (80% of perimembranous and muscular VSDs). Large VSDs cause heart failure and require intervention.

How VSD Works (Pathophysiology)

🔁 Left-to-right shunt
Because left ventricle pressure is higher than right, blood flows from left → right through the hole. This extra volume goes to lungs → pulmonary overcirculation → eventually leads to pulmonary hypertension (Eisenmenger syndrome if untreated).
🧬 Types by location:
• Perimembranous (80%)
• Muscular (5‑20%)
• Inlet (rare)
• Outlet (subpulmonary)
💡 Simple mnemonic: "L→R = Leak to Right" – extra blood goes to lungs.

Signs & Symptoms

👶 Small VSD (often asymptomatic)
• Loud hologystolic murmur (heard best at left sternal border)
• Normal growth, no heart failure
❤️ Large VSD (symptoms appear 4‑8 weeks)
• Tachypnea, respiratory distress
• Poor feeding, failure to thrive
• Diaphoresis (sweating) with feeds
• Recurrent lung infections
• Hepatomegaly, cardiomegaly
📌 Classic murmur: Harsh, loud, holosystolic murmur at left lower sternal border; often with a thrill.

Diagnosis & Tests

  • Physical exam: Murmur, thrill, possible signs of heart failure.
  • Chest X‑ray: Cardiomegaly, increased pulmonary vascular markings.
  • Electrocardiogram (ECG): May show biventricular hypertrophy or left atrial enlargement.
  • Echocardiogram (Doppler): Gold standard – shows location, size, and hemodynamics.
  • Cardiac catheterization: Rarely needed; used to measure pulmonary pressures and Qp/Qs ratio (pulmonary‑to‑systemic flow).
🔍 Gold standard: Echocardiography with color Doppler.

Treatment & Nursing Care

💊 Medical management
• Diuretics (furosemide) for fluid overload
• Digoxin for heart failure
• ACE inhibitors to reduce afterload
• Nutritional support (high‑calorie formula)
⚕️ Surgical closure
Indicated for large VSDs, failure to thrive, pulmonary hypertension. Patch closure (pericardial or Dacron) via open heart surgery. Device closure via catheter for some muscular VSDs.
👩‍⚕️ Nursing priorities: Monitor respiratory status, weight, feeding tolerance; prevent infection; educate parents about signs of heart failure; promote bonding; administer medications accurately.

Complications & Prognosis

  • If untreated large VSD: Congestive heart failure, pulmonary hypertension, Eisenmenger syndrome (irreversible pulmonary vascular disease), infective endocarditis.
  • Prognosis: Most small VSDs close spontaneously by age 2‑5 years. Surgical repair has >95% success rate; children lead normal lives with regular follow‑up.
⚠️ Eisenmenger syndrome: Reversal of shunt (right‑to‑left), cyanosis, clubbing. Prevented by early surgery.

Prevention & Family Education

  • Prenatal: Folic acid (400‑800 mcg), avoid teratogens (alcohol, lithium), control maternal diabetes.
  • Postnatal: Immunize on schedule (including RSV protection for high‑risk), monitor growth, provide high‑calorie feeding, teach signs of worsening heart failure.
  • Endocarditis prophylaxis: Not routinely recommended for isolated VSD unless within 6 months of surgical patch closure or residual defect.
📞 Parent teaching mnemonic “W.H.A.L.E.” – Weight (daily), Heart failure signs (fast breathing, sweating), Alertness (lethargy), Lung infections (prevent), Energy (small frequent feeds).
